The Engineering Goal

Early-stage exoskeleton development needs controlled, repeatable tests. I developed a robotic phantom knee to investigate actuation, sensing and mechanical interaction, with a digital representation connected to the physical system.

The prototype combines antagonistic actuation, an elastic mechanism and pneumatic soft-tissue representation. A Raspberry Pi links the physical platform to Unity through ROS 2, exchanging commands and system feedback.

My Engineering Contribution

  • Design and prototyping: Developed the knee mechanism and custom components using Blender and 3D printing, then integrated the actuators and pneumatic components.
  • Hardware integration: Connected the robotic actuators and distributed sensors, and implemented the acquisition and control pipeline.
  • ROS 2 architecture: Structured nodes for actuator communication, sensing and subsystem integration.
  • Digital twin: Developed the Unity environment and bidirectional connection for control commands and physical-system feedback.
  • Testing: Performed incremental subsystem and partial-system tests, documenting implemented functions and integration limitations.

System Architecture & Technical Decisions

A Raspberry Pi 5 running Ubuntu 24.04 and ROS 2 Jazzy connects the actuators, sensors and Unity digital twin. Dedicated nodes separate subsystem functions so they can be developed and tested incrementally.

Mechanism and antagonistic actuation

Two backdrivable actuators use 19:1 and 5:1 transmission ratios. The second actuator follows the first within an adjustable clamping band. An elastic mechanism provides the basis for investigating compliance and variable-stiffness behaviour.

Pneumatic system

Inflatable chambers introduce a compliant outer layer around the knee structure. A pump, reservoir, valves and pressure sensing support pressure regulation, inflation and controlled air release.

Embedded sensing

Thigh and shin modules use FeatherS3 microcontrollers with ISM330DHCX inertial sensors, MMC5983MA magnetometers and TMP117 temperature sensors. Dedicated ROS 2 nodes bring their data into the control platform.

Communication and Unity interface

Commands travel from Unity to the physical system through ROS–TCP; actuator and sensor feedback update the digital representation. The interface supports state monitoring and experiment parameter adjustment.

Testing, Results & Limitations

The thesis documents subsystem and partial-system evaluation. Findings reported in the official thesis abstract include the following:

Digital communication

Stable ROS–TCP data streaming during subsystem validation.

Pressure control

Closed-loop pressure tracking suitable for bench-top pneumatic trials.

Safety handling

Tests of the E-STOP and release-air functions.

Tracking and clamping

The project demonstration shows adjustment of the relative-motion band from 0° to approximately ±38°.

This is a research prototype. Further biomechanical characterization, stiffness evaluation and comprehensive system-level validation remain areas for development. The thesis provides the experimental setup and limitations.
