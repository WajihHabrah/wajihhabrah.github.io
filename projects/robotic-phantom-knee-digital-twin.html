Master’s Thesis: Robotic Phantom Knee Digital Twin

My master’s thesis project focused on developing a one-degree-of-freedom robotic phantom knee test platform, combining physical actuators, embedded sensors, ROS 2 and a Unity-based digital twin for real-time control, visualization and engineering validation.

Project Overview

The Robotic Phantom Knee is a one-degree-of-freedom physical and digital test platform developed as part of my master’s thesis in Biomedical Engineering at Chalmers University of Technology. The platform was designed to support early-stage investigation and evaluation of exoskeleton and rehabilitation-robotics concepts in a controlled bench-top environment.

The system combines antagonistic robotic actuation, an elastic variable-stiffness mechanism, pneumatic soft-tissue simulation, embedded sensing, ROS 2 communication and a Unity-based digital twin. Together, these subsystems enable physical behaviour, actuator control, sensor feedback and the corresponding digital representation to be studied as an integrated system.

A Raspberry Pi 5 running Ubuntu and ROS 2 provides the central communication and control platform, linking the physical knee mechanism with the Unity environment and supporting real-time exchange of commands and system feedback.

My Contribution

My work included system architecture, hardware integration, actuator communication, sensor acquisition, ROS 2 node development and communication between the physical system and Unity.

I also worked on synchronizing position and velocity data, structuring the ROS communication pipeline and preparing the platform for testing robotic and rehabilitation concepts.

System Architecture

The system follows a bidirectional physical–digital architecture. A Raspberry Pi 5 running Ubuntu and ROS 2 acts as the central integration platform between the Unity-based digital twin and the physical robotic phantom knee.

Dedicated ROS 2 nodes manage actuator communication, pneumatic control, embedded sensor acquisition and application-level control, while the ROS–TCP interface enables real-time communication with the Unity environment.
